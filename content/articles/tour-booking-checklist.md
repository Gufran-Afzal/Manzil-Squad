Paying for a trip is the moment you hand over control. Before that point you can ask any question, compare options and walk away. After it, you depend on the organiser keeping their word. This checklist helps you make sure the important things are settled first.

## Before anything else: is it a real offer?

A post with a mountain photo and a price is an advert, not an offer. A real offer has a date, a price, an itinerary, named suppliers and an organiser you can identify. If the post only says "DM for details" and the details stay vague, keep asking until they aren't, or move on.

## 1. The trip itself

- [ ] **Exact dates**, with departure and return times
- [ ] **Pickup and drop-off point** in Lahore
- [ ] A **day-by-day itinerary**, even a short one
- [ ] What happens if the trip is **delayed or postponed** because of weather

> **Tip:** A trip that leaves Lahore late at night and returns late the next night is a very different experience from one with a night's sleep in between, even if both are called "2 days".

## 2. The price

- [ ] The **price per person**, as a single number, not "starting from"
- [ ] What's **included**: transport, rooms, which meals, entry tickets, activities
- [ ] What's **not included**
- [ ] Whether the price depends on **group size**, and what happens if the group is smaller
- [ ] Whether there are **extra charges** on the trip (jeeps, heaters, tips)

If the answer to "what isn't included?" is "nothing", ask again more specifically. There is almost always something.

## 3. The transport

- [ ] The **company** and **type of vehicle**
- [ ] Whether it is a **dedicated vehicle** for the group or shared
- [ ] Whether there are **two drivers** on long journeys
- [ ] The organiser's rules on **night driving** and bad weather

## 4. The accommodation

- [ ] The **name** of the hotel or guest house (and look it up)
- [ ] **Room sharing**: how many people per room
- [ ] **Separate rooms** for male and female participants
- [ ] **Hot water and heating**, especially outside summer
- [ ] Whether **breakfast** is included

## 5. The people running it

- [ ] The **organiser's full name** and phone number
- [ ] Their **registration** with a provincial tourism authority, and the number
- [ ] **Who travels** with the group, and how many coordinators
- [ ] An **emergency contact** your family can use during the trip

Our guide on [choosing a safe tour operator](/articles/choosing-a-safe-tour-operator.html) explains how to check registration.

## 6. The cancellation and refund policy

Ask these four questions and get the answers in writing:

1. If **I** cancel, how much do I get back, and does that change closer to the date?
2. If **you** cancel, do I get a full refund? How and when?
3. If the trip is **postponed**, can I choose a refund instead of the new date?
4. Can I **transfer my seat** to a friend?

If the organiser doesn't have a policy, that tells you something.

## 7. How you pay

- [ ] The account name **matches** the organiser or their registered business
- [ ] You're paying a **deposit**, not the full amount far in advance, unless you know and trust the organiser
- [ ] You'll get a **written confirmation** that names you, the trip and the amount paid
- [ ] You've **saved screenshots** of the payment and every important message

> **Safety:** Never share card numbers, PINs, one-time passwords (OTPs) or banking passwords with anyone, including a tour organiser. No genuine organiser needs them.

## 8. Your own preparation

- [ ] Your family knows **where you're going**, with whom, and the organiser's number
- [ ] You've told the organiser privately about any **medical needs**
- [ ] You have your **CNIC** and enough **cash**
- [ ] You've read the **packing list** (here's [ours](/articles/northern-pakistan-packing-checklist.html))

## Red flags

Walk away, or at least slow down, if:

- the organiser **won't name the hotel or transport**
- you're pushed to **pay within minutes** "before seats run out"
- the price **changes after you pay**
- the account name **doesn't match** the organiser
- nobody can tell you **who to call in an emergency**

## If something goes wrong

1. **Raise it in writing** with the organiser first, calmly, and keep a copy.
2. **Keep evidence:** payment screenshots, the original advert, and messages.
3. If the operator is registered, you can contact the **provincial tourism authority** that registered them.
4. If you believe it is fraud, report it to the relevant authorities.

> **Verify:** Complaint routes and the agencies that handle them can change. Check the current process with the relevant provincial tourism department.
