Murree is the hill station almost everyone in Punjab has heard of. Nathiagali, about 32 kilometres further along the ridge, is the one people tend to discover second. Both are reachable from Lahore in a weekend, and they share the same pine-covered hills. They feel very different, though, and the right choice depends on what you want from the trip.

## The short answer

- **Choose Murree** if you want a lively town, shopping and food, and you don't mind crowds.
- **Choose Nathiagali** if you want forest, quiet walks and a calmer pace.

For a first group trip focused on the outdoors, we lean towards Nathiagali and Ayubia, and you can pass through Murree on the way.

## Murree: the classic hill station

Murree grew as a colonial-era hill station, and its centre is still the **Mall Road**, a pedestrian street of shops, cafés and food stalls. It's close to Islamabad and Rawalpindi, which makes it the default day trip for millions of people.

That convenience is also its main drawback. On weekends, public holidays and snow days, the roads in and out can be jammed for hours, and the Mall can feel more like a city bazaar than a mountain escape.

**What students like:** the energy, the food, the shopping, and the feeling of being somewhere famous.

**What students don't:** traffic, crowds, and prices that rise with demand.

## Nathiagali: the forest option

Nathiagali sits in the middle of the **Galiyat**, the chain of small resorts between Murree and Abbottabad. It's smaller and greener than Murree, with deodar and pine forest right up to the edge of town. Next door is **Ayubia National Park**, with the Pipeline Track, a mostly level forest walk of about 4 kilometres.

**What students like:** the walks, the views, the quiet evenings and the feeling of actually being in the mountains.

**What students don't:** fewer food and shopping options, and evenings can feel very quiet if you were expecting a lively town.

## Winter: a serious difference

Both places get snow, and snow brings crowds. In January 2022, [23 people died](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Murree_snowstorm) in vehicles stranded during a snowstorm in Murree, after more than 150,000 vehicles entered the area and roads designed for far fewer cars became gridlocked. Some deaths were linked to hypothermia and some to carbon monoxide from exhausts blocked by snow.

Since then, authorities have introduced traffic controls in snow season.

> **Verify:** Check current traffic restrictions, entry limits and weather warnings for Murree and the Galiyat before any winter trip.

Whichever you choose, the lessons are the same: don't drive into heavy snow without confirmed accommodation, keep fuel, food and warm clothes in the vehicle, and never sit in a stationary car with the engine running when it is surrounded by snow.

## Which is better for a group?

For a student group, Nathiagali has some practical advantages:

- **The walking suits groups.** The Pipeline Track is easy enough for everyone, so nobody is left behind.
- **It's easier to keep a group together** in a smaller place than on a packed Mall Road.
- **Evenings are calmer,** which makes headcounts and early starts simpler.

Murree can work well as a short stop on the way: a meal and a walk on the Mall before continuing along the ridge.

## Getting around once you're there

Murree is compact around the Mall, and much of it is walkable, but traffic in and out of town is the main challenge. In the Galiyat, the resorts are strung out along a ridge road, so you either walk between neighbouring galis on forest tracks or drive between them.

For a group with its own coach, this matters less. For independent travellers, it's worth knowing that a car or a hired vehicle makes the Galiyat much easier to explore than public transport does.

## Budget differences

Neither place is automatically cheaper. Murree has more choice at every price level, which helps on a budget, but prices rise sharply with demand on snow days and holidays. The Galiyat have fewer places to stay, so the good ones book up early in summer. In both, the biggest saving for a group comes from sharing transport and rooms, not from the choice of town.

## Can you do both in a weekend?

Yes, as long as you don't try to do too much. A common pattern is to pass through Murree on the way up, stay in the Galiyat, and walk the Pipeline Track the next morning. Trying to fit in shopping in Murree, a hike up Mushkpuri and the chairlift in Ayubia in one weekend usually means spending most of it in traffic.
