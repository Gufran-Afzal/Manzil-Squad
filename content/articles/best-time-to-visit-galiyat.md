The Galiyat (the string of hill resorts between Murree and Abbottabad that includes Nathiagali and Ayubia) can be visited all year. Each season changes what you'll see, what you can do and what you need to prepare for. This guide covers the general pattern; always check the forecast before a specific trip.

## The short version

- **Best all-round:** late spring, early summer and autumn
- **Greenest:** the monsoon, if you don't mind rain
- **Snow:** winter, with serious preparation and flexible plans
- **Busiest:** summer holidays, Eid holidays, long weekends and snow weekends

## Spring (roughly March to May)

Spring starts cold and ends pleasant. Early in the season there may still be snow on higher ground and cold nights; by May, days are comfortable for walking and the forest is fresh.

**Good for:** walking, quieter roads before the summer rush, clear views on dry days.

**Watch out for:** late cold snaps and spring storms. Pack a warm layer.

## Summer (roughly June)

Early summer is when the Galiyat make the most sense to someone coming from Lahore's heat. Days are mild, evenings are cool, and the walks are at their most comfortable.

**Good for:** the Pipeline Track, longer walks, evenings outdoors.

**Watch out for:** crowds during school holidays and Eid, and higher prices for accommodation.

## Monsoon (roughly July to mid-September)

The monsoon brings rain almost every day, sometimes in short heavy bursts and sometimes as cloud that settles into the forest for hours. The hills are at their greenest and the mist moving through the pines is beautiful.

**Good for:** atmosphere, lush forest, cooler days.

**Watch out for:**

- slippery, muddy paths. Shoes with good grip matter more than ever.
- landslides and slow roads, which can delay journeys
- views that disappear into cloud
- leeches on some damp forest trails

> **Safety:** In heavy rain, avoid streams and steep slopes, and allow extra time for travel. Check the Pakistan Meteorological Department forecast and any provincial disaster management advisories before setting off.

## Autumn (roughly late September to November)

After the monsoon, the air often clears, giving some of the best long views of the year. Days are cool and settled, and the crowds thin out after the summer.

**Good for:** clear views, comfortable walking, fewer people.

**Watch out for:** shorter days and cold evenings, especially in November. Plan walks so that you're back before dark.

## Winter (roughly December to February)

Winter in the Galiyat is cold, and heavy snow can fall. Recent winters have tended to bring more snowfall in February than in December or January, but the pattern varies from year to year. Snow transforms the forest and draws large crowds from the plains.

**Good for:** snow, if you are well prepared.

**Watch out for:**

- road closures and traffic restrictions
- icy paths and closed trails
- crowds on snow weekends, especially around Murree
- very cold rooms if heating is limited

The [2022 Murree snowstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Murree_snowstorm), in which 23 people died in stranded vehicles, is a reminder to take winter seriously. In snow: have confirmed accommodation before you go, carry fuel, water, food and warm clothes, follow official traffic advisories, and **never sit in a stationary vehicle with the engine running** when snow is around it.

> **Verify:** Winter traffic rules for Murree and the Galiyat have changed since 2022. Check current restrictions close to your travel date.

## Which season suits a student group trip?

For a first group trip, we prefer **late spring, early summer or autumn**:

- walks are comfortable for everyone
- roads are more predictable
- packing is simpler
- there's less risk of last-minute postponement

A monsoon trip can be wonderful if the group is prepared for rain. A winter trip needs more preparation and a willingness to postpone if the forecast is poor.

## Month-by-month at a glance

| Months | What to expect | Good for a first group trip? |
|---|---|---|
| March–April | Cool to cold, late snow possible on high ground | Sometimes, with warm layers |
| May–June | Mild days, cool evenings, busy holidays | Yes |
| July–mid-September | Monsoon rain most days, lush forest | Only if the group is ready for rain |
| Late September–October | Clear, settled, cooler | Yes, often the best |
| November | Cold evenings, short days | Yes, with an early return each day |
| December–February | Cold, possible heavy snow, road restrictions | Only with careful planning |

These are broad patterns, not forecasts. Two Junes or two Februaries can be very different.

## What the season changes on a trip

The season doesn't only change the weather; it changes the plan.

- **Daylight.** In late autumn and winter, the light goes early. A walk that is comfortable at 3 pm in June may finish in the dark in December.
- **Packing.** A June trip needs a fleece; a January trip needs thermals, gloves and waterproof boots.
- **Travel time.** Rain and snow slow hill roads, so return journeys need more slack.
- **Accommodation.** Heating and hot water matter much more outside summer. Ask about them before booking.
- **Price and availability.** Holiday weekends fill up early and cost more.

## Avoiding the crowds

- Travel on **weekdays** if you can.
- Avoid **Eid holidays** and **long weekends** unless you've booked well ahead.
- Start walks **early in the morning**.
- Stay in the **Galiyat rather than Murree** for a quieter evening.

## Checking the weather properly

Generic weather apps can be unreliable in the mountains. Before a trip, check:

- the [Pakistan Meteorological Department](https://www.pmd.gov.pk/) forecasts
- any advisories from the national or provincial disaster management authorities
- local news for road closures

We do these checks before every departure, and our [trip pages](/trips.html) explain what happens if a trip has to be postponed.
