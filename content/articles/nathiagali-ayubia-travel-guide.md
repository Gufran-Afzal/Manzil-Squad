If you have never been to the northern hills, Nathiagali and Ayubia are one of the gentlest introductions there is. The forests are dense, the walks are short, and you can get there and back from Lahore in a weekend. This guide covers what the area is like, what to do, and what to watch out for.

## Where are Nathiagali and Ayubia?

Both sit in the **Galiyat**, a string of small hill resorts along the forested ridge between Murree and Abbottabad, in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa's Abbottabad District. The word *gali* means a pass or gap in the ridge, and many of the place names end in it: Khaira Gali, Changla Gali, Dunga Gali, Nathia Gali.

Nathiagali sits at about 2,410 metres. Murree and Abbottabad are each roughly 32 kilometres away by road. Ayubia is a few kilometres to the south-west and gives its name to **Ayubia National Park**, established in 1984 and now covering about 3,312 hectares.

To put the height in context: Lahore is at about 217 metres. On this trip you climb more than two kilometres in a day, which is why the air feels so different when you step off the coach.

## What the area is like

The Galiyat are forest first and towns second. The slopes are covered in temperate conifer and broadleaf forest: deodar cedar, blue pine, silver fir, yew, oak and horse chestnut. In the park you may see rhesus macaques, and the park's recorded bird list runs to more than 200 species, including kalij and koklass pheasants. The Indian leopard lives here too, though visitors almost never see one.

The towns themselves are small. Nathiagali has a short bazaar, guest houses and hotels, and **St. Matthew's Church**, a wooden church from the colonial period that sits on the edge of the ridge. Ayubia has a cluster of shops, eateries and the chairlift.

## Things to do

### Walk the Pipeline Track

This is the walk most people come for. The **Pipeline Track** runs for about 4 kilometres through the forest between Dunga Gali and Ayubia, following the line of an old water pipe, which is why it is mostly level. It is shaded, well used and suitable for beginners. Allow time to stop: the views open up where the trees thin out.

> **Tip:** Start early. By late morning on weekends the track can be busy, and the light is better in the morning anyway.

### Climb Mushkpuri Top (if you are fit)

**Mushkpuri** is the second-highest peak in the Galiyat at about 2,800 metres. The route from Nathiagali is a steady climb of about 4 kilometres; the route from the Dunga Gali side is steeper. The meadow at the top is worth it on a clear day. Only attempt it with good shoes, enough water, a clear forecast and plenty of daylight. On a group trip, it should be an optional extra, not the main plan.

### Ride the Ayubia chairlift

The chairlift at Ayubia is often described as the first of its kind in Pakistan. It lifts you above the trees for wide views over the ridges. Whether it is running depends on the day and the season.

> **Verify:** Check that the chairlift is operating, and the current ticket price, close to your travel date. It is not included in most package prices.

### Miranjani: for experienced hikers only

**Miranjani**, at about 2,992 metres, is the highest peak in the Galiyat. It is a longer and harder hike than Mushkpuri and needs an early start, local knowledge and good weather. It is not suitable as a casual add-on.

### Slow down

Part of the appeal is doing very little: walking to a viewpoint, drinking tea while the cloud moves through the trees, and noticing how quiet it is compared to the city.

## When to go

Nathiagali has four quite distinct seasons:

- **Spring and early summer** are fresh and green, and daytime walking is comfortable.
- **The monsoon** runs from about July to mid-September, with rain expected almost daily. The forest is at its greenest, but paths get slippery and views come and go with the cloud.
- **Autumn** usually brings clearer air and cool, settled days.
- **Winter** runs from about November to February. Heavy snow can fall, and recent winters have seen snowfall more often in February than in December or January.

For a first visit with a group, late spring, early summer and autumn are the most forgiving. Read our [best time to visit the Galiyat](/articles/best-time-to-visit-galiyat.html) guide for more detail.

## Getting there from Lahore

The usual route is north on the motorway to Islamabad, then up through Murree and along the ridge road into the Galiyat. On a weekend trip, most of the first day is travel.

> **Verify:** Journey times vary a lot with traffic, weather and roadworks, especially on weekends and in snow. Check the route and current conditions before you set off rather than relying on a fixed estimate.

## Where to stay

There are hotels and guest houses in Nathiagali and nearby galis, ranging from basic to comfortable. For groups, the important questions are less about luxury and more about clarity: how many people per room, whether male and female participants have separate rooms, whether there is hot water, and whether the booking is confirmed in writing.

## Staying safe

- **Weather turns quickly.** Carry a warm layer and a rain jacket, even in summer.
- **Don't feed the monkeys.** Macaques will take bags and food and can bite.
- **Stay on marked tracks** and keep the group together in the forest.
- **In snow, never sit in a parked car with the engine running.** Snow can block the exhaust and let carbon monoxide into the car. This was a factor in the deaths during the [2022 Murree snowstorm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Murree_snowstorm).
- **Respect the park.** It is a protected area. Carry your litter out.

## A simple two-day plan

Our proposed [Nathiagali and Ayubia weekend](/trips/nathiagali-ayubia-weekend.html) follows a shape that works well for first-timers:

1. Leave Lahore early, arrive in the afternoon, and take an easy evening walk around Nathiagali.
2. Walk the Pipeline Track the next morning, spend some free time in Ayubia, then head home.
