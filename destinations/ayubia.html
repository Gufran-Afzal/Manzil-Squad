About Ayubia

Ayubia National Park was established in 1984 and covers about 3,312 hectares of temperate conifer and broadleaf forest. It includes the small resorts of Khaira Gali, Changla Gali, Khanspur and Ghora Dhaka.

For most visitors, Ayubia means the Pipeline Track: a shaded forest walk of about 4 km towards Dunga Gali. The park is also home to rhesus macaques, more than 200 recorded bird species and, rarely seen, the leopard.

What to see

Pipeline Track
About 4 km, mostly level, through deodar, blue pine and oak forest.
Ayubia chairlift
Described as Pakistan's first facility of its kind. Check that it is operating on the day you visit.
Birdwatching
The park's species list includes kalij and koklass pheasants. Early mornings are best.

Seasons

Late spring to autumn is the most comfortable walking season. Winter can close or ice over trails.

  • Monsoon months bring lush forest but slippery paths and leeches on some trails.
  • In winter, check whether the chairlift and the Pipeline Track are open before travelling.

Travel advice

  • Start the Pipeline Track early to avoid crowds.
  • Carry water; there are few reliable stops on the track itself.
  • Keep food out of sight. Macaques will take bags.

Safety

  • Do not feed or approach monkeys.
  • Stay on the marked track and keep the group together.
  • Carry litter out with you: the park is a protected area.
