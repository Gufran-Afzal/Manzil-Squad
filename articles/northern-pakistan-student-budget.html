Planning and budget

How to plan a Northern Pakistan trip on a student budget

A practical method for budgeting a trip north: what the costs are, where the money goes, how groups cut the cost per person, and the false savings to avoid.

A trip north doesn't have to be expensive, but it is easy to overspend without noticing, and easy to "save" money in ways that make the trip worse or less safe. This guide gives you a method rather than a list of prices. Prices change every season, and a figure copied from a blog post written two years ago is a bad base for a budget.

Start with the shape of the trip

Before you think about money, decide three things:

  1. How many nights. Each extra night adds accommodation and food, but also spreads the fixed cost of getting there over more days.
  2. How far. The Galiyat work as a weekend from Lahore. Swat, Naran or Hunza need several days, and the travel itself becomes a large part of the cost.
  3. How many people. For a group, this is the single biggest lever on cost per person.

Know your four big costs

Almost every trip budget breaks down into the same four parts.

1. Transport

For a group from Lahore, this is usually a hired coach or van, priced for the whole vehicle rather than per seat. That's why group size matters so much: a coach costs roughly the same whether it carries 25 people or 45, so the cost per person drops as the group grows.

Things that change the transport price: the size and condition of the vehicle, the number of days it is hired for, fuel prices, tolls, and whether the driver's food and accommodation are included.

2. Accommodation

Usually priced per room per night. Sharing rooms is the most effective saving. The main questions are how many people per room, and whether the price includes extra mattresses, heating in winter, and breakfast.

3. Food

Often the most underestimated cost, because it is spent in small amounts. Decide in advance which meals are included in the group price and which people buy themselves.

4. Activities and entry

Chairlifts, jeep rides to lakes, entry tickets and guides. These vary a lot by destination. Jeep hire, in particular, can be a large share of a Naran or Swat trip.

Check before you travel

Get current written quotes for each of these before you set a price. Fuel, tolls and hotel rates can change between seasons.

The contingency line matters. Something always costs more than expected: a road detour, an extra meal, a broken heater that means moving rooms.

How groups save money (properly)

  • Book the whole vehicle and fill it. An empty seat costs the same as a full one.
  • Share rooms sensibly. Three or four to a room is common for student groups, as long as it is stated clearly in advance.
  • Travel off-peak. Weekdays and the shoulder seasons are cheaper and less crowded than long weekends and public holidays.
  • Buy food as a group. One agreed dinner order is often cheaper than everyone buying separately.
  • Keep the itinerary realistic. Cramming in extra destinations adds fuel, time and stress.

False savings to avoid

Some savings make a trip worse, or less safe:

  • An unsuitable vehicle. Hill roads are steep and long. A cheap vehicle in poor condition is not a saving.
  • Overnight driving in bad weather to save the cost of a night's accommodation.
  • Unclear rooming. A cheaper rate that turns out to mean eight people in one room causes problems that cost more to fix on the day.
  • No contingency. If there is no buffer, the first surprise comes out of someone's pocket.
  • Paying an unknown operator in full upfront because their price is lower. Read what to check before paying for a tour package.

Your personal spending money

Even on a package trip, you will spend money that isn't in the price: snacks on the road, tea at viewpoints, souvenirs, optional activities. Decide your personal limit before you leave and carry some cash. Card machines and ATMs are less common in small hill towns, and mobile signal can be patchy.

Tip

Keep cash in two places, so losing one bag doesn't leave you with nothing.

What a fair package price covers

When you see a package price, check which of the four big costs it includes. A lower price that excludes meals and activities may cost you more in the end than a higher one that includes them. A clear operator will tell you exactly what is and isn't included, in writing.
