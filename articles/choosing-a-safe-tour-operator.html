How to choose a safe group tour operator in Pakistan

How to check a tour operator before you trust them with your money and your safety: registration, written details, vehicles, rooming, payments and warning signs.

Check before you travel

Seasonal and practical details in this article were last reviewed on 26 October 2026. Prices, road conditions and rules change; confirm anything important with an official source close to your travel date.

Group tours advertised on social media are one of the most popular ways for students to travel north. Many are run well. Some are not: the bus is different from the one advertised, the hotel has changed, the group is twice the size, or the organiser stops answering after the trip. This guide sets out what to check before you pay anyone, including us.

1. Check whether they are registered

Tourism businesses in Pakistan are regulated by provincial authorities, and the rules depend on where the business is based and where it operates.

  • In Punjab, the Department of Tourist Services licenses travel agencies, tourist guides, hotels and restaurants under laws that date from 1976 and were amended in 2020. See the DTS Punjab website (opens in a new tab).
  • In Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, which includes the Galiyat, Swat and Kaghan, tourism businesses including tour operators are licensed through the Culture and Tourism Authority's online system. In September 2026 the provincial tourism authority announced that tour operators and travel agents must register before providing tourism services in the province, and advised the public to avoid unregistered operators (ProPakistani report (opens in a new tab)).

Ask the operator directly: Which authority are you registered with, and what is your registration number? A legitimate operator will answer without hesitation.

Check before you travel

Registration rules and the way the public can check a licence change over time. Confirm the current process with the relevant provincial tourism department before relying on a registration number.

2. Get the details in writing

Before paying, you should have a written confirmation (a WhatsApp message is fine, as long as it's specific) that includes:

  • the exact dates and departure and return times
  • the price per person and what it includes and excludes
  • the transport company and type of vehicle
  • the name of the hotel or guest house, not just "a hotel in Nathiagali"
  • room sharing: how many people per room, and whether male and female participants have separate rooms
  • the cancellation and refund policy
  • the organiser's full name and a working phone number

If an operator won't put these in writing, treat that as your answer.

3. Ask about the vehicle and driver

The journey is often the riskiest part of a trip north. Ask:

  • What type of vehicle is it, and how old?
  • Is the driver experienced on hill roads?
  • Will there be a second driver for long journeys?
  • Do they drive overnight? In what weather will they not travel?

It's reasonable to ask for the vehicle's registration number before departure.

4. Ask who is responsible on the trip

  • Who travels with the group, and how many coordinators are there for the number of participants?
  • What happens if someone is unwell or injured?
  • How do they keep track of everyone at stops?
  • Who can parents contact during the trip?

A good operator has thought about these questions before you ask.

5. Look at how they talk about prices and availability

Be cautious of:

  • Pressure tactics: "Only 2 seats left!" every time you look, or countdown timers on every post
  • Vague prices: "Starting from…" with the real price only revealed after you've paid a deposit
  • Prices far below everyone else's with no clear explanation
  • "Hotel to be confirmed" right up to departure

Honest operators tell you which details are confirmed and which are not.

6. Check their track record

  • Look for photos and reviews from real participants, with names you can recognise, not just stock photos.
  • Ask a friend or senior who has travelled with them.
  • Check how they respond to complaints in comments.

Be aware that reviews can be faked. A small number of genuine, specific reviews is more convincing than hundreds of vague five-star ones.

7. Pay safely

  • Pay to an account in a name you can match to the operator.
  • Keep screenshots of the payment and the written confirmation.
  • Prefer paying a deposit to paying the full amount weeks in advance, unless you trust the operator.
  • Never share card details, PINs, OTPs or passwords with anyone.

Warning signs

  • Refuses to name the hotel or transport company
  • Asks for the full payment immediately
  • Changes the price after you've paid
  • Can't explain who to contact in an emergency
  • Pushes you to decide within minutes

A checklist you can copy

  • Registration authority and number
  • Exact dates and times in writing
  • Price per person, inclusions and exclusions
  • Hotel name and room sharing
  • Transport company and vehicle
  • Cancellation and refund policy
  • Coordinator names and emergency contact
  • Payment to an account that matches the operator
