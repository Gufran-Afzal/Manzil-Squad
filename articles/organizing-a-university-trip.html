How to organise a university trip without the stress

A practical plan for students organising a class or society trip north: timeline, budget, permissions, roles, money handling and what to do on the day.

Organising a trip for your class or society sounds fun until you're holding thirty people's money, chasing a coach company at midnight and answering the same question in four WhatsApp groups. It doesn't have to be like that. Here's a plan that keeps it manageable.

Start six to eight weeks out

Most stress comes from leaving things too late. A rough timeline:

  1. Six to eight weeks before: choose the destination and dates, check the academic calendar, and get any university permission you need.
  2. Five to six weeks before: get quotes for transport and accommodation, and set a price.
  3. Four weeks before: open registrations and collect deposits.
  4. Two weeks before: confirm final numbers with suppliers and collect the balance.
  5. One week before: share the final itinerary, packing list and rooming list.
  6. Two days before: check the weather and road advisories, and confirm everything with suppliers again.

Check the rules first

Before you announce anything:

  • Find out whether your university needs to approve trips run in its name or by a society, and what it needs from you (a faculty adviser, a form, parental consent for under-18s).
  • Agree whether it is an official society trip or an informal trip among students. This affects who is responsible.

Check before you travel

Policies differ between universities and change over time. Ask your student affairs office for the current rules.

Pick a realistic destination

For a first group trip, choose somewhere that:

  • can be done in the time you have without driving through the night
  • has easy walking that everyone can manage
  • has accommodation for the whole group in one place

The Galiyat work well for a weekend. Places like Swat or Naran need more days. See Murree vs Nathiagali for a comparison of two weekend options.

Build the budget from real quotes

Get at least two written quotes each for transport and accommodation. Then:

  • divide shared costs (the coach, for example) by a conservative number of participants, not the most optimistic
  • add a contingency of around 10%
  • decide clearly what's included and what people pay for themselves

Our student budget guide includes a worksheet you can copy.

Split the jobs

One person trying to do everything is how trips go wrong. Share the roles:

RoleResponsible for
Lead organiserSuppliers, final decisions, the overall plan
TreasurerCollecting money, keeping records, paying suppliers
CommunicationsOne announcement channel, answering questions
Safety leadEmergency contacts, headcounts, first-aid kit, weather checks
RoomingRoom lists and keys

Agree in advance who makes decisions on the day if plans have to change.

Handle money carefully

Money is where most disputes start.

  • Use one account for the trip and keep it separate from personal money.
  • Record every payment received: name, amount, date, method.
  • Give each person a written confirmation when they pay.
  • Write the refund policy down before collecting money, and stick to it.
  • Keep receipts for everything you pay out, and share a simple summary of spending with the group afterwards.

Communicate in one place

Create a single announcements-only WhatsApp group where only organisers can post, and a separate chat for questions. Pin the key information:

  • departure time and exact pickup point
  • itinerary
  • packing list
  • organisers' phone numbers
  • the rooming list (shared privately if needed)

Collect the right information, and no more

You need each participant's name, phone number, an emergency contact, and any medical information relevant to their safety. Collect sensitive information privately, store it carefully, and delete it after the trip. You don't need CNIC copies from everyone unless a hotel or authority actually requires them.

On the day

  • Arrive early at the pickup point with a printed list of names.
  • Do a headcount before leaving and at every stop. Use a buddy system so people notice if a friend is missing.
  • Brief the group before departure: timings, rules, what to do if someone gets separated.
  • Keep emergency numbers on paper as well as phones.
  • Check weather and road updates during the trip, and be willing to change plans.

Safety

If conditions turn bad, the right decision is usually to slow down or turn back. A shorter trip is always better than an unsafe one.

A one-page plan you can share

Before you open registrations, write a single page that everyone can read. It saves dozens of repeated questions:

  • destination, dates and departure and return times
  • price per person and what it includes
  • payment method, deadline and refund policy
  • what to bring
  • organisers' names and numbers
  • rules for the trip (timings, staying with the group, no alcohol)

Pin it in your announcement group and send it to anyone who asks. If something changes, update the page and say clearly what changed.

After the trip

  • Share a spending summary and settle any refunds quickly.
  • Ask for feedback: what worked, what didn't.
  • Thank your suppliers if they were good, so you can use them again.
  • Delete personal data you no longer need.

Or let someone else do it

If organising sounds like more than you want to take on, you can join an organised trip instead, or ask an organiser to plan one for your class or society. We're happy to talk about group trips for your class or society.
