Why student group trips can be a great way to explore Pakistan

The real advantages of travelling north with a student group, the trade-offs, and how to get the most out of a group trip.

Most people in Lahore grow up hearing about the north (the forests, the lakes, the snow) long before they see it. For many students, the first chance comes at university. Travelling with a group isn't the only way to go, but it's often the easiest first step. Here's why, and what to keep in mind.

It makes the trip affordable

The biggest costs of a trip north are shared costs: hiring a vehicle and renting rooms. A coach costs much the same whether it carries 25 people or 45, so a full group brings the cost per person down a long way. Shared rooms do the same for accommodation.

For students paying from pocket money or part-time work, that's often the difference between going and not going. Our student budget guide explains how this works.

Someone else handles the planning

Planning a trip for a few friends is manageable. Planning transport, rooms, meals and an itinerary for thirty people is a job in itself. On an organised group trip, someone else is responsible for:

  • finding and checking the transport and accommodation
  • working out the route and timings
  • keeping track of the group
  • deciding what to do when the weather changes

You still need to pack well and turn up on time, but you can spend the trip enjoying it instead of managing it.

It's often easier for families to say yes

For many families, especially for a first trip, "I'm going with an organised group, with coordinators, separate rooms, and a written plan" is a much easier conversation than "a few of us are going to figure it out."

A good organiser makes this easier by sharing the itinerary, the place you're staying and a contact number that parents can call.

You meet people outside your usual circle

Group trips mix people from different departments, years and sometimes universities. Long coach journeys, shared meals and walking together are a natural way to meet people, and many friendships made on trips last well beyond them.

You see more with people who know the area

A group led by organisers who have planned the route can take you to walks and viewpoints you wouldn't find from a quick search, and can steer you away from the crowded, overpriced spots.

There's safety in a well-run group

Travelling in an organised group isn't automatically safer, as it depends entirely on how the group is run. But a well-run group has advantages that are hard to match on your own:

  • regular headcounts, so nobody is left behind
  • people responsible for checking weather and road advisories
  • a plan for what happens if someone is unwell
  • strength in numbers on forest tracks

Read how to choose a safe group tour operator for what a well-run group should look like.

The trade-offs

Group travel isn't for everyone, and it's worth being honest about the downsides.

  • Less flexibility. The group moves together, on a schedule. If you want to spend three hours photographing one view, a group trip may frustrate you.
  • Shared rooms. You may share with people you don't know well.
  • Group pace. Walks are chosen to suit everyone, so very fit hikers may want more.
  • Noise and energy. A coach full of students is rarely quiet.

If these matter to you, a smaller trip with friends might suit you better. If it's your first time north, though, the trade-offs are usually worth it.

What to ask before you join one

Not every group trip is run with care. Before you sign up, it's fair to ask the organiser:

  • What exactly is confirmed, and what is still being arranged?
  • Who travels with the group, and how many coordinators are there?
  • Where will we stay, and how many people share a room?
  • What happens if the weather turns, or the trip is postponed?
  • How do I pay, and what's the refund policy?

A good organiser answers these in writing without hesitation. Our guide to what to check before paying for a tour package has a full checklist.

How to get the most out of a group trip

  1. Read the itinerary before you go, so you know what to expect each day.
  2. Pack properly. Most discomfort on trips comes from being cold or wet. See our packing checklist.
  3. Be on time. On hill roads, a late start can mean driving in the dark.
  4. Talk to people you don't know. That's half the point.
  5. Tell the coordinators privately about anything that could affect your safety.
  6. Look up from your phone. The signal is patchy anyway.

Starting close to home

You don't need to go to Hunza for your first trip north. The Galiyat, a few hours beyond Islamabad, offer pine forests, cool air and easy walks in a weekend.
