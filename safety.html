Safety and participant responsibilities

What we do to keep trips safe, and what we ask of everyone who travels with us.

Mountain trips are usually safe and very enjoyable, but the hills are not Lahore. Weather changes quickly, roads are steep, and help can be further away. This page sets out what we commit to and what we ask of you. It is a draft and will be reviewed before our first trip.

What we do

Before the trip

  • Check transport and accommodation suppliers before booking (see how we plan).
  • Check weather forecasts and official advisories in the days before departure, and postpone if conditions are unsafe.
  • Share the itinerary, pickup point, packing list and coordinator contact numbers with every participant.
  • Collect an emergency contact for each participant, privately (not through the website form).

During the trip

  • At least [two] coordinators travel with each group. [Confirm the ratio of coordinators to participants.]
  • Coordinators take a headcount every time the group moves on.
  • Separate rooms for male and female participants.
  • A basic first-aid kit travels with the group. [Confirm whether a coordinator holds a first-aid certificate before stating it.]
  • We do not drive through the night in winter snow conditions.
  • Walks are chosen to suit the whole group. Harder hikes are optional and only in good weather.

What we ask of you

  • Stay with the group, especially on forest tracks and in crowded places. Tell a coordinator before you go anywhere on your own.
  • Be at meeting points on time. The coach can't wait long on hill roads.
  • Follow coordinators' instructions about safety, even if you disagree; talk to them about it afterwards.
  • Do not approach or feed wildlife. Macaques in Ayubia can bite and will take food.
  • Stay away from river edges, cliff edges and unfenced viewpoints, especially in rain.
  • No alcohol, drugs or weapons on our trips.
  • Treat other participants, local people and the places we visit with respect.
  • Tell us privately, before the trip, about anything that could affect your safety on a walk or a long journey (for example a medical condition or a fear of heights). You don't need to share details through the website.

In an emergency

Coordinators will contact local emergency services first and then your emergency contact.

Check before you travel

Rescue 1122 is the emergency service number in Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and 130 is the National Highways and Motorway Police helpline. Confirm both numbers and add the nearest hospitals to each trip briefing before departure.

Winter travel

In January 2022, people died in vehicles stranded in a snowstorm in Murree after far more cars entered the area than its roads could handle. In heavy snow we will not travel into crowded hill areas, and we will never leave participants in a stationary vehicle with the engine running, because snow can block the exhaust and let carbon monoxide into the vehicle.

Monsoon travel

Rivers in the north can rise suddenly after rain upstream, even when the weather where you are standing looks calm. On our trips, nobody goes into or onto the edge of a river during the monsoon.
